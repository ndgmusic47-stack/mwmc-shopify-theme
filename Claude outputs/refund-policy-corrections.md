# Refund policy — two corrections

Nathan confirmed: **South Thames Trading Company Ltd** is the legal entity.
"Sultan of Statues" is a trading name of it, as "Mum Where's My Cards" was.

The policy editor in the Shopify admin is in a closed shadow root and would
not take an automated edit, so these two changes need doing by hand. They
take about 30 seconds.

**Admin → Settings → Policies → Return and refund policy**

---

## Change 1 — the trading name

Find:

> This policy applies to retail purchases from **Mum Where's My Cards**, a
> trading name of South Thames Trading Company Ltd.

Replace with:

> This policy applies to retail purchases from **Sultan of Statues**, a
> trading name of South Thames Trading Company Ltd.

The legal entity is unchanged. Only the trading name moves.

## Change 2 — the trading-card sentence

In the "Returning an item" section, find:

> You may inspect an item as you would in a physical shop. We may reduce a
> refund where handling beyond what is reasonably necessary has diminished
> the item's value. **Opening trading-card packs, boxes or tamper-evident
> product seals can significantly reduce their value.**

Replace the bolded sentence with:

> **Removing a statue from its original box and inner packaging, or breaking
> factory seals, can significantly reduce its value.**

This sentence is what lets you reduce a refund when a returned piece comes
back unboxed. As written it refers to trading cards and would not obviously
apply to a resin statue, so it is worth getting right before anyone returns
a £1,000 piece.

While there, update "Last updated: 6 September 2026" to today's date.

## Left alone deliberately — worth your judgement

These are unchanged. None is wrong exactly, but each is now a slightly odd
fit, and they touch consumer rights rather than branding.

| Item | Current state | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Contact email | `hello@mumwheresmycards.com`, twice | Still works while that domain stays connected, but reads oddly on a Sultan-branded policy |
| Return postage | "you are responsible for the return postage" | You are collection-only, so a change-of-mind return is more likely to be hand-delivered |
| Delivery refund clauses | Refund includes "least expensive standard delivery option"; express not refundable | No delivery options exist right now, so these are dormant until you add courier rates |
| "Cancelling before dispatch" | Written around dispatch | Nothing is dispatched under collection-only |
| 14-day cancellation right | Standard distance-selling wording | An online order collected in person is still a distance contract, so the right generally applies — but the mechanics in the policy assume posting |

I am not a lawyer and did not rewrite these. Once courier delivery is set
up most of them stop being awkward on their own. If you want one thing
looked at professionally before volume picks up, make it the interaction
between collection-only and the 14-day cancellation mechanics.
